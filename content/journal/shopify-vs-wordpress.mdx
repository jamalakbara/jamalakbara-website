If you're starting an online store from scratch and want your team to handle products and orders without managing a server, I'd start with Shopify. If you already have a WordPress site your team uses every week, I'd check whether WooCommerce can add the store without forcing you to rebuild the rest of the site.

WooCommerce is the plugin that turns a WordPress site into a store. That's the comparison here: a hosted Shopify store versus a WordPress site with WooCommerce on a host you choose. [WooCommerce pricing and hosting overview](https://woocommerce.com/pricing/)

## Who runs the store after launch?

On Shopify, hosting is part of the subscription. Your staff can add products, check stock, and fulfill orders from the Shopify admin. Someone still has to manage the theme and apps, check that integrations work, and keep shipping rules up to date. [Shopify plans and features](https://www.shopify.com/pricing)

With WooCommerce, products and orders live in the same WordPress admin as your pages and posts. That can be convenient if your team already works there. Decide who will handle backups, WordPress and plugin updates, and checkout testing after changes. A managed host may cover some of that work. Ask whether it will also check that a customer can still pay and that the right shipping rate shows up. [WooCommerce update guide](https://woocommerce.com/document/how-to-update-woocommerce/)

## Which costs should you compare?

- **Shopify:** Add the plan, any paid apps, theme or build work, and payment costs. If you sell in person, check whether you need paid point-of-sale features or new hardware.
- **WordPress with WooCommerce:** The core plugin is free. Add hosting, any paid extensions and their renewals, theme or build work, payment processing, and whoever keeps the store running.

Use the same sales assumptions for both. A monthly plan is easy to compare on paper, but it tells you little about the cost of a returns app or the time your team spends reconciling stock. Check plan and extension pricing for the features you'll actually use. [Shopify pricing](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) · [WooCommerce pricing](https://woocommerce.com/pricing/)

## How I'd choose for three small businesses

**A clothing brand selling online and at weekend markets.** I'd try Shopify first if the team wants one place to track stock across the website and market stall. Before buying hardware, I'd test an in-person sale, a return, and what happens when the last item sells. [Shopify POS](https://www.shopify.com/pos)

**A workshop business with an established WordPress site and a small line of products.** I'd test WooCommerce on a copy of the current site. If the theme works with it, shipping is straightforward, and someone already looks after WordPress, adding a shop could be a smaller project than moving the whole site.

**A wholesaler with customer-specific prices or unusual shipping rules.** I'd test those requirements on both platforms before choosing. Ask a real buyer to place a sample order with the right price and shipping method. Find out whether the setup needs a higher plan, an extension, or custom work, and who will maintain it later.

## Try a normal day before you commit

Give the person who will run the store a sample product. Have them change the price, place a test order, process a refund, and update stock. Write down where they get stuck and who they'll call if checkout breaks on a Friday. Those answers are a better basis for a platform decision than a feature list alone.

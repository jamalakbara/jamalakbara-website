For a custom business site or store with a handful of page types, I quote about one month, and between 4 and 6 weeks once the scope grows. Most of that time isn't typing code. It's the prototype, your feedback, and waiting for content.

Those numbers come from two recent quotes of mine. One was a catalogue site rebuilt and migrated to WordPress, estimated at 4 to 5 weeks. The other was a custom Shopify storefront with six app integrations, estimated at 5 to 6 weeks. They're estimates, and I'll say what moves them.

## The phases, in working days

Here is the catalogue rebuild, which is the simpler of the two:

| Phase | What happens | Working days |
|---|---|---|
| Analysis and URL mapping | Audit the old site, list every page and address, plan the redirects | 2–3 |
| Design and prototype | Clickable mockups of the main page types for you to approve | 4–5 |
| Platform and theme setup | Install, set up the theme structure and essential plugins | 2–3 |
| Development | Build the pages from the approved prototype | 7–9 |
| Review and revisions | You review, one round of feedback, then refinements | 2–3 |
| QA and go-live | Device and browser testing, redirects, handover | 2–3 |

The Shopify store runs on the same skeleton, with longer prototype and development phases (5–7 and 10–15 days) and a separate 3–5 day step for configuring the apps. That's why it lands closer to six weeks.

## Why the prototype comes first

I don't start building until you've approved a prototype. It looks like an extra step, and it saves time. Changing a layout in a design file takes an hour. Changing it after it's built takes a day, and it often breaks something next to it.

## What stretches the schedule

The estimates assume three things, and they're the usual reasons a project runs long:

- **Slow feedback.** The calendar keeps moving while a prototype sits waiting for approval. A week of silence adds a week.
- **Content that isn't ready.** Product photos, page text, and prices have to exist before I can place them. A site built around placeholder text gets built twice.
- **Access.** I need your hosting and domain details to set up and to go live. I start once the deposit, access, and first content are in.

Scope changes matter too. A new page type or an extra integration in week three is a new quote, not a free addition.

## What a faster project looks like

Smaller jobs, like a one-page landing page, are shorter, and I'll give you a specific date in the estimate, not a range from a template. If you need something quickly, the fastest way to get it is to bring finished content and agree on one person who gives feedback within a couple of days.

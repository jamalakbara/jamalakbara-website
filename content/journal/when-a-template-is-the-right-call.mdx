I build custom sites for a living, so you'd expect me to tell you templates are a trap. They aren't. Plenty of businesses should use one, and I'd rather say so than sell you a build you don't need.

The mistake isn't using a template. It's using one without knowing what it costs you later.

## When a template is the right call

**You're testing an idea.** If you don't yet know whether anyone wants what you sell, a site that exists this week beats a beautiful one that exists in two months. Spend the money on finding out.

**Your site has one job and it's simple.** A name, a few services, a phone number, a contact form. If the page doesn't need to do anything clever, a template does it fine.

**You'll edit it yourself, often.** Website builders are good at this. If your team changes prices on Tuesdays and you don't want to ask anyone, a builder with a friendly editor may serve you better than a custom build.

**It has a short life.** An event, a campaign, a one-season promotion. Don't build something permanent for something temporary.

**The budget is the budget.** A good template site is better than no site, and much better than a custom site you can't finish paying for.

## When it stops working

**Your brand is part of the product.** If people choose you partly because of how you look and feel, a template that looks like a thousand others works against you.

**You need something it doesn't do.** Custom booking logic, a particular checkout flow, an integration with how your business actually runs. Past a certain point you're fighting the template, and plugins and workarounds add weight and risk.

**It's slow and you can't fix it.** Template sites often load code for features you never use. If speed matters, because you pay for ads or sell on phones, that overhead is a real cost.

**You've outgrown the structure.** The sign is a sentence like "we'd love to do X but the site can't." That's a template talking.

## The test I'd use

Write down what the site must do in one sentence. Then ask two questions:

1. Can a template do exactly that, without workarounds?
2. In a year, will I still want only that?

Two yeses: use a template, and be happy about it. A no on either: it's worth a conversation about something custom.

## What I'd still check on a template

- **Ownership.** Can you export your content and move if you leave the platform?
- **Speed on a phone.** Open the demo on a mid-range device, not on your laptop.
- **The real cost.** Subscription, paid apps and plugins, and your own time, over a year, not just month one.

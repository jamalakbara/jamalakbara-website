A custom [small business website](/services/business-website) starts at **$900 USD** when I build it. That covers a few pages with custom design, a mobile-friendly layout, a contact form, and two rounds of revisions. Domain registration and hosting are separate. A single landing page can cost less. A store or a site that needs custom booking will cost more. I give you a fixed quote once we agree on what the site needs to do.

If you're comparing quotes, start with the first-year total. A low build price can come with a monthly support fee, while a higher quote might include writing the pages and moving over your old site. Get both quotes on the same footing before you decide.

## What changes the build price?

- **One landing page.** If you have one offer and want visitors to call or request an estimate, one page may be enough. The work grows if you need copy written, several versions for ad campaigns, or leads sent to a CRM.
- **A small service website.** A few pages can explain what you offer, show past work, and give customers a way to reach you. Ask how many pages the quote includes and who will supply the copy and photos. Five pages that share a layout take different work from five pages that each need their own design.
- **A redesign.** My [website redesign work](/services/website-redesign) starts at **$1,200 USD** for a small business site. Part of the job is deciding what to carry over, especially pages customers already find through search. Ask whether the quote covers content migration and redirects for URLs that change.
- **A store or booking site.** Linking to a booking service is a smaller job than building a booking flow with deposits and cancellation rules. For a store, budget for product setup, shipping, and checkout testing as well as the storefront design.

## What will you pay after launch?

Check the renewal price for the domain and the regular price for hosting or the site builder. List any paid tools the site relies on, such as scheduling software, email, or store apps. If you're selling online, keep payment fees separate from fixed costs since they vary with sales.

For example, Shopify lists its U.S. Basic plan at **$29 USD per month when paid yearly**, or **$348 USD for a year** at that rate. That's the platform subscription. Add your build quote, domain, any paid apps, and applicable payment fees to get a fuller picture. [Shopify's U.S. pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/pricing)

Maintenance is a separate line to ask about. Find out who handles a broken form, software updates where needed, or a new page after launch. My small business website builds include a month of fixes and support after launch. If you need ongoing help, discuss what that would cover and how it would be billed.

## Compare the first year and the renewal year

For each quote, add the build fee, one-time extras such as copywriting or migration, and twelve months of recurring charges. Then run the same calculation for year two with the renewal rates and any support you expect to keep. For a store, estimate variable fees using the same sales assumptions for each option.

Before signing, ask who owns the domain and platform accounts, what your team can edit, and what you can take with you if you move the site. Those answers matter when you need to change providers or update something in a hurry.

If you found me from outside Indonesia, you probably have two questions before you get to the work: *where is Bandung* and *what's it like working across that distance?* Fair. Here's the honest answer, including what it doesn't solve.

Bandung is a university city and the capital of West Java, a few hours from Jakarta by road, and I work from here. Most of my clients are remote. The about page says "Remote Worldwide" next to Bandung because that's how it actually runs.

## The time zone, plainly

Bandung is UTC+7 with no daylight saving. In practice:

- **Europe:** a few hours of live overlap. When it's mid-afternoon with me, it's the morning with you.
- **Australia:** easy. You're a few hours ahead of me or close to it.
- **US:** little real-time overlap. Early morning for you is evening for me. It works, but it works through writing, not meetings.

So I don't pretend we'll be on call together all day. I design the process so you don't need that. We have one call at the start, and almost everything else happens in writing.

## How the work runs

1. **A kickoff call** about the goal, the people who'll use the thing, and the deadline.
2. **Scope and price in writing** before any work begins. I price by project, not by the hour, so you know the number up front.
3. **Regular updates you can click through.** A live link, not a screenshot and a promise. You see the thing working as it gets built.
4. **Handover.** The code goes into your repository and you own it. Nothing is locked in a tool only I can use.

Written updates sound slower than a meeting. In my experience they're faster, because you answer when you're at your desk and I don't wait for a slot in your calendar.

## What it costs

My prices are in US dollars. A small business website starts from $900, a focused React or Next.js build from $2,500, and a redesign from $1,200. Payment is 50% upfront and 50% before launch. Hosting and domain are paid separately, and I'd rather you register both in your own name. The [cost breakdown](/journal/how-much-does-a-website-cost) shows what moves a quote up or down.

## What distance doesn't fix

Being remote doesn't help if you need someone in your office on Thursday, or a team of ten on a short deadline. It also doesn't help if you want an answer in five minutes during your day and I'm asleep. If real-time access is the thing you value most, hire someone in your time zone. I'd rather tell you that now than discover it in week three.

## The good part

Most of what makes a project go well isn't geography. It's a clear brief, one person who answers for the result, and updates you can trust. I do both design and code, so there's no handoff between two people for a message to get lost in.

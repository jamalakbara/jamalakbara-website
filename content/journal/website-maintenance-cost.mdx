My maintenance costs IDR 2.5 million per month, and every project I build includes the first month free. That number only means something once you know what it covers, so I'll start there, then list the costs that sit outside any maintenance plan.

## What maintenance should cover

I treat maintenance as keeping the site you already have working. In the free month after launch that means:

- **Minor bug fixes.** A form that stops sending, a button that breaks on one phone.
- **Small layout adjustments.** A section that needs a tweak now that real content is in.
- **Updates.** On WordPress, plugin and platform updates, which need testing after they run.
- **Troubleshooting** anything inside the scope I delivered.

Paid maintenance covers the same kind of work. If you're comparing plans, ask for this list in writing. "Maintenance" with no list is where most disagreements start.

## What it doesn't cover

- **New features and new pages.** That's a new project with its own quote. A maintenance plan that quietly includes unlimited new features is priced for that, or won't deliver it.
- **Problems at your host or a third-party service.** If the hosting company has an outage or an app changes its rules, I can help you sort it out, but the cause isn't mine to fix.
- **Content writing and photography.**

## The costs outside maintenance

People look at the maintenance number and miss these, so ask about them before you sign anything:

- **Hosting and domain.** Billed by the provider, usually yearly.
- **Platform subscription.** On Shopify, that's the plan you choose. In my last quote I pointed one client to the Grow plan billed yearly at about US$49 a month, and warned that on monthly billing the same store costs more. Check the current price before you budget.
- **Paid apps and plugins.** A shipping or loyalty app can charge monthly. One shipping app in that quote started from IDR 99,000 a month.
- **Payment gateway fees.** A percentage of each sale, charged by the gateway.

These bills show up whether or not anyone maintains the site. Ask for them as a list, with who pays each one.

## Do you need a plan?

If your site is a one-page landing page that rarely changes, you may not need monthly maintenance. A one-off fix when something breaks can cost less than a plan you never use.

If your team sells through the site, runs on WordPress, or depends on apps that update often, maintenance is cheaper than the day your checkout stops working and nobody knows who to call. Updates left for months pile up, and a big overdue update breaks more than a small recent one.

## Questions to ask any developer

1. What exactly is included each month, in writing?
2. How fast do you respond when something breaks?
3. Who pays for hosting, the domain, and apps, and when do they renew?
4. What counts as a new feature, and how is that priced?
5. Can I leave with my files and accounts in my own name?

The last one matters most. Your site should live in accounts you own.
